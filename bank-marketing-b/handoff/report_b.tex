% Method
\subsection{Logistic Regression and Gaussian-Prior MAP}
Let $x_i$ denote the processed features for record $i$, and let $y_i=1$
indicate a term-deposit subscription. With $z_i=b+x_i^\top\beta$, the model
assigns subscription score $\sigma(z_i)=(1+\exp(-z_i))^{-1}$. We compare
two estimates of the same coefficients by minimizing
\begin{equation}
J_{\lambda}(b,\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\bigl[\log(1+\exp(z_i))-y_i z_i\bigr]
+\frac{\lambda}{2}\Vert\beta\Vert_2^2.
\label{eq:b-penalized-nll}
\end{equation}
Setting $\lambda=0$ gives the maximum likelihood estimate (MLE).
For $\lambda>0$, independent $\mathcal{N}(0,\lambda^{-1})$ priors on the
slopes and a flat prior on the intercept give the maximum a posteriori
(MAP) estimate. The slope gradient is $X^\top(p-y)+\lambda\beta$ and the
intercept gradient is $\sum_i(p_i-y_i)$, where $p_i=\sigma(z_i)$. We
implement the objective and gradient and solve both fits with L-BFGS.

Both fits use the same training-derived numeric scaling and categorical
encoding. We omit the first category in sorted order for each field so
that the intercept and all category indicators do not encode the same
information. The two logistic designs have 41 columns. The Gaussian
naive Bayes baseline retains its original 50-column encoding.

% Experiments and Results
\subsection{Effect of the Gaussian Prior on Subscription Prediction}
We use the 45,211 records from UCI \texttt{bank-full.csv}~\cite{moro2014bank}
and the same stratified 70/15/15 split as the Gaussian naive Bayes
baseline (seed 42). All three models exclude last-call duration, which
becomes available after the call. Inside the training set, five
stratified folds with seed 42 select $\lambda$ by mean trapezoidal PR-AUC.
Each fold fits the encoder and scaler using its training portion.
We refit on all 31,648 training records and independently choose each
model's cutoff by maximizing F1 on the 6,782 validation records.

The mean fold PR-AUC was 0.40617 for MLE and peaked at 0.40658 among the
positive values at $\lambda=10$. Stronger priors gave 0.40516 at
$\lambda=30$ and 0.39669 at $\lambda=100$. The selected prior reduced
the slope norm from 3.771 to 3.454. For example, the previous-campaign
success coefficient fell from 2.267 to 2.093 log-odds relative to failure.

Both logistic fits achieved higher test PR-AUC than Gaussian NB
(Table~\ref{tab:b-test} and Fig.~\ref{fig:b-test}). Their PR curves nearly
overlapped. At the cutoffs selected on validation data, MAP classified
163 additional records as yes. Of these, 35 were subscribers and 128
were false positives. Among records with cellular contact, the
false-positive rate rose from 10.20\% to 13.48\%, and the false-negative
rate fell from 53.15\% to 48.20\%. At a shared cutoff of 0.5, MAP
produced 7 fewer false positives and 11 more false negatives than MLE.

In the original CSV, record 45133 was a false positive for both fits.
Previous-campaign success raised its fitted log-odds by 2.267 under MLE
and 2.093 under MAP. Both fits missed subscriber 17874, whose 29 contacts
in the current campaign contributed roughly $-2.49$ to both scores.
MAP recovered subscriber 40887, whose log-odds moved from $-1.629$
under MLE to $-1.502$ under MAP, clearing the MAP cutoff of $-1.576$.
The additional false positive at record 42626 illustrates this cost.
On this random split, shrinkage left the test ranking nearly unchanged.
Its effect on errors varied with the cutoff. Performance on later
campaigns remains untested.
